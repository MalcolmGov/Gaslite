The strategic story in one paragraph

Marlin Moodley today is a trusted, advice-led practice that serves clients over email and phone, and requests quotes from insurers manually. The opportunity is not “a website, an app and a chatbot.” It is to build a digital insurance intermediary platform — a quotation-orchestration and AI layer beneath a single customer experience — so a client can start one conversation on any channel and the platform gathers their needs, obtains comparable quotes from multiple insurers (in real time where APIs exist, through automated workflow where they don’t), and hands the advisor a complete, compliant file to advise on. Advice stays human. Everything around it becomes instant.

What we propose

The Marlin Moodley Digital Insurance Platform — Launch Edition: one build that puts the quote journey live on all three channels in 12 weeks. Decision 1

Three-tier insurer connectivity

Integrate insurers at whatever level they support — real-time API, digital/batch, or automated email/portal workflow — so launch never waits for the slowest insurer. The customer sees one experience.

Decision 2

Compliance by design

FAIS, POPIA, TCF and the incoming COFI framework are designed into journeys, data and AI guardrails from day one — turning regulation into a trust advantage rather than a retrofit.

Decision 3

AI assists, humans advise

AI agents handle information, intake, comparison, documents and follow-up. Suitability advice, claims outcomes and any decision with significant customer effect stay human-owned and auditable.

02Current-state assessment

A new practice with unusual clarity: broad insurer access already contracted, a compliance home at Masthead, and no legacy book to migrate. The digital estate, however, is a brochure — not a platform — and the growth engine the practice plans (bought leads at volume) has nothing to land on yet.

Strengths
  • Advice-led trust: advising since 2004; positioning as “Senior Executive Financial Advisor”.
  • Insurer access already contracted: life agency codes with Momentum, Discovery, Old Mutual, BrightRock, Bidvest Life, PPS, Sanlam and Liberty; short-term with Momentum, Discovery, Old Mutual, Santam, Hollard, CIA, iTOO, One, Elite, Echelon and Brolink.

Constraints

  • Capacity ceiling: growth is currently bounded by the advisor’s and assistants’ inboxes and calendars — untenable at the planned ±50 quotes/week from bought leads.

Direct insurtechs sell one balance sheet. Aggregators sell leads. An FSP-backed advisory platform can do what neither can: compare across insurers and advise — with a licensed human accountable for suitability. The ASISA gap study shows the market’s problem is not appetite but access and advice capacity. A platform that lets one advisor run hundreds of concurrent, compliant conversations attacks exactly that constraint.

Capturing even a microscopic share of a R50.4-trillion cover gap is a life-changing book of business for a boutique practice. Design principles

Journeys are designed once as conversational flows, rendered on web, WhatsApp and app. A client can start on WhatsApp and finish with the advisor without repeating themselves.

AI prepares; the licensed advisor advises. Every client-facing AI output is explainable, logged and escalation-ready — the FAIS-safe posture and the brand promise.

Every interaction enriches Customer 360 — powering renewals, cross-sell, retention and prediction. The data asset is the long-term moat.

07Channel strategy — web, WhatsApp, app

Sequence matters: web/PWA rebuilds credibility and captures search demand; WhatsApp meets clients where they already are; the native app rewards the established base. All three share the same journeys and core.

Website / PWA — the credibility and conversion engine (Weeks 3–9)

Experience

  • Outcome-led product pages for every line (incl. the missing short-term pages), calculators (life-cover gap, income-protection), and educational content targeting protection-gap search terms.
  • Digital needs-analysis wizard → instant Tier A quotes or tracked quote case; secure client portal: policies, documents, claims status, renewals, secure messaging.
  • PWA: installable, offline-tolerant, push-capable — 80% of app value at 20% of app cost, available from day one.

Foundation

  • Full FAIS/POPIA disclosure set, cookie/consent management, complaints journey, advisor credentials.
  • Analytics with funnel tracking from first visit to policy issue; A/B testing on needs-analysis entry points.
  • Local SEO (practice location, reviews) + structured data for financial services.

WhatsApp — the servicing and reach channel (Weeks 5–9)

Journeys

  • Enquire & quote: guided needs capture, document upload (ID, licence, existing schedules), quote status, appointment booking.
  • Service: policy documents, beneficiary updates, claims intake (FNOL), premium queries, renewal confirmations.
  • Advisor handover at any moment — the advisor sees full conversation context in the workspace.

Economics & compliance

  • Meta’s July 2025 per-message pricing makes customer-initiated service conversations effectively free, with modest per-template fees for utility and marketing messages. A service-led strategy is structurally cheap to run.
  • POPIA s69: broadcast/marketing only to opted-in contacts, opt-out honoured in-channel; full conversation archival for FAIS record-keeping.

Mobile app — the loyalty channel (Phase 2)

Native app (single codebase, iOS + Android) once the base justifies it: biometric login, portfolio dashboard across all policies and investments, document vault, claims tracking, renewal nudges, secure chat. Success metric is engagement of existing clients — not downloads. The PWA carries acquisition until then, and the app reuses its journeys and APIs, which is why it lands in Phase 2 rather than the launch build.

Future voice channel: the conversational architecture (intent → journey → core APIs) is voice-ready by design; a phone/IVR AI concierge can be added later without re-platforming.

Section 09 · Platform

09AI strategy — agents, intelligence, analytics

AI is the force multiplier that lets one advisor run hundreds of concurrent, compliant client journeys. Every agent operates inside explicit FAIS/POPIA guardrails: AI prepares and predicts; the advisor advises and decides.

Customer-facing

AI Insurance Concierge

24/7 first responder on web & WhatsApp: explains products factually, answers questions, collects needs-analysis information, books advisor appointments, hands over seamlessly. Never recommends products.

Customer-facing

Quote & Application Agent

Turns a completed needs analysis into structured quote requests across all tiers; prepares applications; identifies missing information and chases it conversationally.

Customer-facing

FNOL / Claims Intake

Guided claims first-notification on WhatsApp/web: captures incident details, documents and photos; creates the case; sets expectations. Claims decisions remain with insurer and advisor.

Back-office

Document Intelligence

Reads IDs, bank confirmations, existing policy schedules and emailed insurer quotes (the Tier C unlock); extracts structured data with human verification on low-confidence fields.

Advisor-facing

Advisor Copilot

Pre-meeting client briefs from Customer 360, side-by-side quote summaries with factual differences, drafted (advisor-approved) records of advice, and after-meeting action lists.

Advisor-facing

Knowledge & Policy Assistant

Retrieval-based assistant over insurer product rules, policy wordings and regulatory guidance — answers cite their sources, so the advisor verifies in one click.

Intelligence

Renewal & Retention

Predicts lapse/churn risk from payment, engagement and life-event signals; triggers advisor-approved outreach before the client shops elsewhere. Renewals become campaigns, not surprises.

Intelligence

Next-Best-Action

Surfaces gap-based opportunities per client (e.g. cover ratio vs. ASISA benchmarks, life events, missing product lines) ranked by likelihood and value — feeding the advisor’s day, not spamming the client.

Intelligence

Predictive analytics

Funnel conversion, quote-to-bind rates by insurer/segment, channel economics, workload forecasting — the management dashboard for running the practice as a platform.

Launch Edition scope: structured journeys with a tightly guard-railed assistant, plus Tier C document parsing. The full agent suite below — copilot, retention, next-best-action, predictive analytics — is the Phase 2 roadmap, enabled by the same architecture.

The guardrail model — designed for FAIS & POPIA

AI can

  • Explain and educate factually; compare factual product attributes
  • Collect information and prepare needs analyses & applications
  • Summarise documents, meetings and portfolios; identify missing information
  • Assist advisors, trigger workflows, draft for human approval

AI does not, independently

  • Provide financial advice or product recommendations to clients
  • Make suitability determinations or material underwriting judgements
  • Decide claims outcomes
  • Take any solely automated decision with significant customer consequences (POPIA s71)

Every AI interaction is logged with inputs, outputs and model version; algorithm performance is monitored and reviewed per FSCA Board Notice 194 of 2017 requirements for automated advice tooling (Section 11). Confidence thresholds route uncertain cases to humans by default.

10Customer 360 & Advisor Copilot

The quiet engine of the whole strategy: one longitudinal record per client, and a workspace that turns it into advisor leverage.

Customer 360 — the single client record

What it holds

  • Identity & KYC/FICA status; consent and communication preferences (POPIA-grade, per purpose)
  • Household & financial profile from needs analyses; life events
  • Every policy across every insurer — cover, premiums, renewal dates, beneficiaries
  • Full interaction history across web, WhatsApp, app, email and meetings
  • Documents: schedules, records of advice, claims files, wills & trusts

What it enables

  • “Never repeat yourself” journeys across channels
  • Cover-gap analysis per client against ASISA benchmarks — the advice conversation starter
  • Renewal, retention and Next-Best-Action intelligence (Section 9)
  • FAIS record-keeping and TCF evidence as a by-product of normal work, not an admin burden

Advisor workspace — a day in the life

08:00 — Copilot morning brief: 3 renewals at churn risk, 2 completed needs analyses awaiting advice, 5 quote cases (1 Tier C overdue — chaser already sent), today’s meetings with prepared client briefs.

10:00 — Client meeting: side-by-side quote comparison on screen; Copilot drafts the record of advice from the meeting; advisor edits, approves, signs — filed to Customer 360 automatically.

14:00 — WhatsApp handover: concierge escalates a complex estate-planning enquiry with full context; advisor answers in-thread; case created.

16:30 — Pipeline view: every open case by stage and SLA; management dashboard shows quote-to-bind by insurer and channel economics.

The workspace includes case/workflow management (every quote, claim and service request is a tracked case), a compliance cockpit (outstanding disclosures, advice records, consent status), and campaign tooling for advisor-approved outreach. The CRM is custom-built on the platform’s own data model: no per-seat licence fees, one schema shared by journeys, quotes and conversations (WhatsApp threads natively, no connector), and full POPIA data control. Its v1 scope is deliberately lean — pipeline, client record, case queue, inbox, documents, tasks — fixed with a written “not in v1” list during Sprint 0; Customer 360 intelligence is its natural Phase 2 evolution rather than an integration project.

11Compliance by design — FAIS · POPIA · TCF · COFI

In financial services, compliance is not a constraint on the platform — it is the product’s trust layer. Every journey is designed against the regulatory frame below, validated with Masthead’s compliance function during Sprint 0.

FrameworkWhat it requiresHow the platform embeds it
FAIS Act (37 of 2002) + General Code of Conduct“Advice” is defined broadly — any recommendation, guidance or proposal on a financial product. Factual information is excluded, but journeys that steer product choice can constitute advice, triggering suitability duties (GCoC s8) and record-keeping.Hard separation between factual/educational content (AI-allowed) and advice (advisor-only); digital needs analysis captures client circumstances before advice; records of advice generated, approved and archived per interaction.
FSCA BN 194 of 2017 (automated advice)Defines “automated advice” and requires FSPs using algorithms to monitor and review algorithm output, quality and suitability.AI guardrail model (Section 9): no autonomous advice; algorithm logging, performance monitoring, human review cycles and documented sign-off built into the AI layer.
POPIA — s69, s71 + general conditionsElectronic direct marketing needs opt-in (or existing-customer exception with opt-out everywhere); no solely automated decisions with significant effect; lawful, minimal, secure processing.Purpose-level consent registry; marketing sends gated on consent state; s71-sensitive decisions always human-in-the-loop with right to make representations; encryption, retention schedules, subject-access tooling.
TCF — six outcomesFair culture; products meet identified needs; clear information; suitable advice; products perform as expected; no unreasonable post-sale barriers.TCF is instrumented, not filed: journey analytics evidence clear disclosure; needs-based product matching; claims/servicing SLAs; complaint journeys in every channel; management TCF dashboard.

The IFWG Regulatory Sandbox is available if any journey (e.g. instant bind on WhatsApp) benefits from supervised live testing.

Section 12 · Trust

12Security architecture

The platform will hold identity documents, financial profiles and health-adjacent information. Security is engineered to financial-services grade from the first sprint — it is also a POPIA condition, not just good practice.

Identity & access

  • Customer identity with OTP + optional biometric (app); step-up authentication for sensitive actions
  • Advisor/staff SSO with MFA; role-based least-privilege access; full admin audit trail
  • WhatsApp number-verification bound to the client record before any personal data is shared in-channel

Data protection

  • Encryption in transit (TLS 1.2+) and at rest; field-level encryption for ID numbers and financial data
  • Data residency in-country or approved region per POPIA s72 transfer rules
  • Purpose-bound retention schedules; secure deletion; documented operator agreements with every processor (cloud, AI, WhatsApp BSP)

Application & AI security

  • OWASP-aligned secure SDLC; dependency and secret scanning in CI/CD; penetration test before each major launch
  • AI-specific controls: prompt-injection defences, PII minimisation to model providers, output filtering, no client data used to train third-party models

Operations & resilience

  • Centralised logging and alerting; incident-response runbook including POPIA breach notification duties
  • Backups with tested restore; RPO/RTO targets defined in Sprint 0; DDoS and WAF protection at the edge
  • Quarterly access reviews; annual security assessment aligned to the licence holder’s requirements

16Investment — build & run costs

A total build investment of R400,000 — fixed, delivered in 12 weeks, with a flat R30k per month for hosting, support and maintenance from launch. Four scope decisions make the number possible; the figures match Quotation № MM-2026-01 and are fixed on acceptance.

The four scope decisions

1 · The PWA is the app

One installable Progressive Web App — home-screen icon, push, offline tolerance — delivers “web” and “app” from one codebase. An app-store wrapper is available as a priced option; the native app proper is Phase 2.

2 · One journey, rendered twice

Needs capture → quote → follow-up is designed once as a conversational flow, delivered on web/PWA and on WhatsApp via the Business API. No per-channel builds — the trick that keeps three channels in one budget.

3 · Integrate the contracted panel

A launch panel of up to 5 short-term + 5 life insurers, drawn from the practice’s existing agency codes and integrated at whatever tier each supports; every other insurer rides the automated Tier C email workflow. Further integrations are priced add-ons, never blockers.

4 · Own the back office

A custom-built CRM is the advisor workspace — pipeline, client record, case queue, WhatsApp inbox threaded per client, encrypted document vault. No licence fees, one data model, and reusable IP; the AI suite and Customer 360 intelligence build on it in Phase 2.

17Business case & KPIs

The investment pays back through four compounding levers. Each has a measurable KPI tree the platform reports natively.

Lever 1

Advice capacity

Automating intake, quoting and admin typically releases 40–60% of advisor time. Same advisor, several times the concurrent client journeys — capacity that currently caps revenue.

KPIs: needs analyses started/completed per month · quote cases per advisor-hour · time from enquiry to advice meeting.

Lever 2

Conversion & speed

Quotes in minutes instead of days collapses drop-off between enquiry and comparison — the leakiest part of today’s email funnel.

KPIs: enquiry→quote rate · quote→bind rate by insurer & segment · median time-to-first-quote (target: minutes for Tier A; <24h Tier B; SLA-met % Tier C).

Lever 3

Retention & wallet share

Renewal intelligence and Customer 360 turn lapse-risk into pre-emptive conversations, and cover-gap analysis into structured cross-sell.

KPIs: policy lapse rate · renewals retained % · products per client · NBA actions taken/converted.

Lever 4

New digital demand

WhatsApp + instant quotes open segments the practice could never economically serve by email — volume that compounds the book.

KPIs: new clients by channel · CAC by channel · WhatsApp journey completion % · portal/app monthly active clients.

Illustrative payback framing

A brokerage book earns recurring commission on premiums under advisement. If the Launch Edition enables the new practice to build R300k–R500k in annual recurring commission within 18 months of go-live — modest capture at the planned ±50 quotes/week from bought leads — the R400k investment recovers in roughly 10–16 months, while building a sellable, systematised asset: a platform-enabled book is worth a multiple of an inbox-dependent one. Sprint 0 replaces this framing with a model built on actual lead costs, conversion and commission rates.

Governance cadence

Monthly platform scorecard (KPIs above + TCF dashboard + cost-to-serve), quarterly strategy review against this document, and a post-launch investment gate before Phase 2 — so spend follows evidence, not momentum.
